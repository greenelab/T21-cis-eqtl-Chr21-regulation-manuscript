## Introduction {.page_break_before}

The triplication of human chromosome 21 (HSA21), Trisomy 21 (T21), causes Down syndrome (DS). 
Multiple studies report that genes on HSA21 express on average at the expected 1.5-fold change when compared to people with euploid karyotypes (disomic 21, D21)[@doi:10.1038/s41588-023-01399-7; @doi:10.1126/sciadv.adg6218; @doi:10.1016/j.celrep.2022.111883; @doi:10.1038/s41467-024-49781-1]. 
A small subset of HSA21 express more closely to the euploid levels. 
Potential causes for lower-than-expected HSA21 gene expression in a cohort study of bulk RNA expression in people with DS include confounding cell type proportions, genetic effects, and compensatory regulation. 
This study assesses the genetic effect hypothesis while controlling for cell type proportions.

Researchers have previously discussed how differences in genetic composotion may affect clinical and molecular phenotypes in DS. A review by Antonarakis et al. noted that ~80% of T21 conceptuses are lost during pregnancy and inferred that a specific combination of genetic variants allow T21 conceptuses to survive to term [@doi:10.1038/s41572-019-0143-7]. 
Rachubinski et al. hypothesized that genetic variations beyond T21 may explain the development of autism spectrum disorder in some individuals with DS. [@doi:10.1002/PD.4957]. 

However, few have tested the genomic hypotheses. 
Valentini et al. assessed genomic effects effects with a genome wide association study (GWAS) in a cohort of 84 children with DS and found PNLPA3 rs738409 variants on HSA22 were associated with liver steatosis [@doi:/10.1016/j.numecd.2020.05.012]. 
Additionally, Hunter et al. tested whether the genotype of common expression quantitative trait loci (eQTLs) explained lower-than-expected gene expression of HSA21 genes in a child with DS compared to family members (i.e., brother, mother, and father euploid for HSA21)[@doi:10.1186/s12915-023-01700-4]. 
The authors concluded that this result demonstrated a lack of gene expression compensation in the genes with expression more similar to those with D21.  

Hunter et al. introduced methodology to control for common factors that bias differential expression studies in DS research. 
Specifically, the authors accounted for genes with no reads, low gene expression, and high genomic repeats. 
Additionally, they reran the common differential expression pipeline in DESeq2[@doi:10.1186/s13059-014-0550-8], which is designed for disomic karyotypes, to account for the T21. 
Overall, the analysis confirmed that the majority of HSA21 genes show the expected 1.5-fold change.There were 5 genes that fell below the expected fold change. 
Of those 5 genes, 3 had common eQTLs in the general population. 
Genotyping confirmed that allele rates in those eQTLs explained the lower-than-expected gene expression in the individual with DS. 


Here we extend the methodology of Hunter et al. to the population level. 
We assess how cis-eQTLs on HSA21 affect whole blood gene transcription in a large cohort of individuals with and without DS. 
We account for triploidy and cell type composition in the differential expression anlaysis. 
We perform ploidy correct genotyping of HSA21 in people with T21 to accurately represent allele counts. 
We compare the sample genotypes against common eQTLs found in whole blood of disomic individuals in the GTEx cohort. 
The results confirm the findings of Hunter et al. - allele frequencies in common eQTLs explain the majority of lower-than-expected gene expression in people with DS.
