## Abstract {.page_break_before}

Down syndrome (DS) results from trisomy of human chromosome 21 (HSA21). 
On average, genes on HSA21 are overexpressed at the expected 1.5-fold increase. 
A small subset of HSA21 genes is expressed closer to euploid controls. 
Could local genetic regulatory variation explain deviations from the expected dosage? 
Using the largest DS-specific transcriptomic dataset to date from the Human Trisome Project (HTP) and GTEx expression quantitative trait loci (eQTLs), we test whether local genetic regulatory variation contributes to gene-specific buffering or amplification of HSA21 dosage effects in DS. 

We compared the bulk RNAseq profiles of protein-coding genes from whole blood samples of 304 people with DS and 95 people diploid for HSA21. 
We filtered out differentially expressed genes with low gene counts, high genomic repeats, and fold changes less than 1 within-cohort SD. 
We further filtered out genes that were not significantly different after ploidy-normalization to find 21 differentially expressed HSA21 genes (6 high and 15 low). 
Consistent with the transcriptomic tissue, we cross-referenced GTEx whole blood eQTLs with the differentially expressed genes. 
We found that 18 genes had cis-eQTLs (cis window ±1Mb, nominal p-value < 1e-4, ZBTB21, LTN1, RUNX1 did not have eQTLs). 
Thus, we called HSA21 variants with ploidy-aware genotyping in 302 people with T21 from the gene expression cohort for comparison.  
We found that all the higher-than-expected gene expression (TSPEAR, MX1, RIPK4, COL6A2, CYYR1, YBEY) was explained by increased representation of minor alleles, serving as a positive control. 
Among the genes with lower-than-expected expression, 9 had a genetic explanation (OLIG2, COL6A1, PDE9A, RBM11, PCBP3, ICOSLG, KCNE1, PRDM15, OLIG1). 
The low gene expression for 3 genes did not have a genomic explanation (BACE2, ADARB1, NRIP1).
